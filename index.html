02 / The thinking

Good maths. Honest limits.

Not all age comparisons tell the same story.

01

Seven isn't magic.

Multiplying every cat year by seven assumes ageing happens at a constant speed. A one-year-old cat is already much more mature than a seven-year-old child.

02

Start with real records.

We anchor the endpoints to Creme Puff's 38 years and 3 days and Jeanne Calment's 122 years and 164 days — both verified by Guinness World Records.

03

Make room for growing up.

We treat the world-record percentage as the baseline, then add an early-maturity adjustment that fades away. At age two, our illustrative analogy is 17 human years — closer to a teenager, not 24.

THE GENTLE MATURITY-ADJUSTED MODEL

H = rC + BC · e−C/3 · (1 − C/Cmax)

C = cat age in years. Cmax = 38.008 and Hmax = 122.449 are the verified records. r = Hmax/Cmax ≈ 3.22165.

B ≈ 10.8519 is calculated to make H(2) = 17. The extra term boosts younger ages but fades exponentially (with a 3-year decay scale), returning to the baseline by older ages and exactly matching the record at 38.008.

The teenage analogy and decay scale are explicitly chosen modelling assumptions, not measurements from a veterinary study. The pure record-percentage alternative is H = rC.

Is this more accurate? It's more defensible than the naive 7× rule because it models faster early maturation and has explicit, verifiable endpoints. But it's not scientifically proven more accurate than veterinary age charts. Maximum observed lifespan is an extreme outlier, not a physiological measure of ageing, and the size and speed of our early-maturity adjustment are modelling choices, not clinical measurements.

WHY HEALTHCARE MATTERS

Ageing isn't the only reason cats die.

Chronic kidney disease is common in older cats. It's usually not curable, but supportive management and therapeutic renal diets can make a meaningful difference for some cats.

One study, cited by the ISFM consensus guidelines, reported median survival of:

Conventional diet264 days
Therapeutic renal diet633 days

A comparison from one study — not a promise, a randomised care/no-care experiment, or proof that all feline deaths are preventable. Treatment choices depend on veterinary assessment, appetite, quality of life and individual circumstances.
